Effective date: [effective date] · Extended Family Integrated Services, LLC

This notice describes how medical information about you may be used and disclosed and how you can get access to this information. Please review it carefully.

Extended Family Integrated Services, LLC ("Extended Family," "we," "us") is required by law to maintain the privacy of your protected health information ("PHI"), to give you this notice of our legal duties and privacy practices, and to follow the terms of the notice currently in effect.

1. How we may use and disclose your information

We may use and share PHI without your written authorization for the following purposes.

Treatment

We use and share PHI to provide, coordinate, and manage the services you receive. Example: our direct support staff review your individual support plan, and we share relevant information with your supports coordinator, your county program, or your health care providers so that your services work together.

Payment

We use and share PHI to bill for and be paid for services. Example: we send service records to the Pennsylvania Department of Human Services (including the Office of Developmental Programs), your county program, or other payers, and use state systems, to confirm authorization and receive payment. Health care operations

We use and share PHI to run our organization. Example: quality review, staff training and supervision, compliance audits, licensing and monitoring reviews, and business planning.

2. Other permitted or required disclosures

The law allows or requires us to share PHI without your authorization in these situations, limited to what is needed:

  • As required by law, including Pennsylvania incident-management and abuse and neglect reporting requirements.
  • Public health activities, such as preventing disease or reporting adverse events.
  • Abuse, neglect, or domestic violence, reported to the appropriate protective services or government authority.
  • Health oversight, such as audits, inspections, investigations, and licensing by state and federal agencies.
  • Judicial and administrative proceedings, in response to a court order or valid legal process.
  • Law enforcement, in limited circumstances allowed by law.
  • Coroners, medical examiners, and funeral directors, and organ and tissue donation.
  • Research, when approved through a process that protects your privacy.
  • To prevent a serious threat to health or safety.
  • Specialized government functions and workers' compensation.
  • Business associates who perform services for us under a written agreement requiring them to protect your information.
  • People involved in your care, such as family members or caregivers, when you agree or do not object, or when it is in your best interest and you cannot tell us.

5. Your rights

  • Get a copy of your records. You may inspect and receive a copy of your PHI, including an electronic copy where we keep it electronically, and ask us to send it to someone you choose. We will respond within 30 days (one 30-day extension is allowed). We may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee.
  • Ask us to correct your records. If you believe information is wrong or incomplete, you may ask us to amend it. We may deny the request in certain cases, and you may then submit a statement of disagreement.
  • Ask for confidential communications. You may ask us to contact you in a particular way or at a particular place. We will accommodate reasonable requests.
  • Ask us to limit what we share. You may ask us to restrict certain uses and disclosures. We are not required to agree, except that we must agree to a request not to share information with a health plan for payment or operations when you have paid for the item or service in full out of pocket.
  • Get a list of disclosures. You may request an accounting of certain disclosures of your PHI we made in the six years before your request.
  • Get a paper copy of this notice, even if you agreed to receive it electronically.
  • Be told of a breach. We will notify you if your PHI is affected by a breach of unsecured information.
  • Choose someone to act for you. A legal guardian or someone with your health care power of attorney may exercise your rights for you. We will confirm that person's authority first.

To use any of these rights, contact our Privacy Officer below.

6. Our responsibilities

  • We must protect the privacy and security of your PHI.
  • We must give you this notice and follow what it says.
  • We will not use or share your information other than as described here unless you tell us we can in writing.
